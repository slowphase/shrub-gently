Before you begin

Welcome

Somewhere inside your little FM-1 there is now a synthesiser that grew out of Serum, one of the most loved sound-design instruments of the last fifteen years. Steve Duda, who made Serum, wrote a free firmware for the FM-1 called Shrub (on screen and in its own manual it's written SHRUB). It turns the box into a wavetable synth: a machine for building sounds from scratch, one decision at a time.

This book teaches you to make those decisions. You don't need to know anything about synthesisers or music theory. You need curiosity, a quiet half hour, and something to listen on.

The promiseWhat you'll be able to do

By the end of Lesson 8 you'll have built a small sound bank: ten of your own patches, saved, named and sorted, including a soft pluck, a deep bass, a slowly drifting pad, a wide shimmering synth, a rhythmic echoing pattern and a gust of wind. More importantly, you'll know why each one sounds the way it does, so when you imagine a sound you'll have a good idea which knobs will take you there.

This book doesn't try to cover every feature Shrub has. Its official Operation Guide does that well. This book picks the dozen ideas that matter most and gives each one the time to sink in.

What you needEquipment

  • An M-VAVE FM-1 running Shrub {{FIRMWARE}}, charged. The lessons were written for this version. If yours shows a different number (Meet the FM-1 shows where to look), most things will still match, but small details may differ.
  • Headphones or speakers. The FM-1's built-in speaker is fine for Lesson 1, but low basses and stereo width only really come alive on headphones or monitors.
  • No computer for the lessons. Every step happens on the FM-1. A computer with Chrome or Edge is only needed to install or update Shrub, or if you'd like to use the optional browser editor later.
  • For the last part of Lesson 8 only: your Akai MPC Sample and a cable with a 3.5 mm stereo plug at one end and two 6.35 mm (1/4 inch) plugs at the other.
Not running Shrub yet?Shrub installs from a web page at shrub.audio, over a USB cable, using Chrome or Edge on a computer. Follow the official instructions there, close any other music software first, and leave the cable alone until the installer says it has finished (about two minutes). If an install is ever interrupted, the official advice is simply to run the installer again: it carries on where it stopped. You can return to M-VAVE's own firmware at any time with M-VAVE's updater. Please use only these official routes.

Where your sounds will live

Shrub has 128 memory slots. Slots 1 to 100 hold the factory presets; slots 101 to 128 start empty, labelled [[~INIT]]. Each lesson saves into the next empty slot, so by the end your sounds sit together:

Already used some of these slots?If you've saved sounds of your own before, some of slots 101 to 110 may not be empty. A slot you've saved into shows a U before its number (U103) and keeps the name you gave it. Before every save, read the top right of the SAVE page: only press [[SAVE]] again when it says [[~REPLACES INIT]]. If it names one of your sounds, turn [[@KNOB 1]] (SLOT) to the next slot that says [[~REPLACES INIT]], and use that number wherever the book says 101 to 110. Jot your numbers down as you go.

Meet the FM-1

The FM-1 is small enough to hold in one hand, and Shrub uses every control on it. This tour takes about ten minutes. You don't need to memorise it: each lesson introduces the controls again as you need them.

1 · Left knobs
[[@MASTER]] is the volume. [[@PRESETS]] browses sounds from anywhere. [[@SELECT]] turns pages. [[@ALGORITHM]] is the “type” knob: it chooses a wavetable, a filter type, an effect's mode and so on.
2 · Octave
[[OCT-]] and [[OCT+]] shift the keys down or up, up to three octaves each way. A lit button tells you you're away from the middle.
3 · Screen
Shows where you are and what the four screen knobs are doing.
4 · Screen knobs
The four knobs at top right, [[@KNOB 1]] to [[@KNOB 4]], change whatever the bottom of the screen shows. Their jobs change on every page.
5 · Buttons
Twelve buttons. Most open a section of the synth; the rest save, play and record.
6 · Keys
27 keys. They don't respond to how hard you press: every note plays at the same strength.

The knobsSeven endless knobs and a volume

Apart from MASTER, the knobs turn forever in either direction: there's no end stop, because the screen keeps track of the value. Turn slowly for fine changes, one small step per click; flick them and they speed up. When you change something with named choices, like a wavetable, its name flashes up large on the screen for a moment.

The screenEvery page has the same three layers

Coloured pills across the top show the parts of the section you're in, with the current one lit. The little dots beside the title show how many pages that part has, and which one you're on.

The buttonsSections, and how to move around

Shrub's whole synth is divided into sections, each behind a button. Press a section button to open it. Press the same button again to step to that section's next part. This is the most useful habit in the book.

Four rules cover nearly all navigation:

  1. Press a section button until the right pill is lit. The steps in this book always say which pill you're aiming for (“press [[EDIT]] until [[~OSC A]] is lit”), because the FM-1 sometimes reopens the part you visited last.
  2. [[@SELECT]] turns the pages of the part you're on. Watch the dots.
  3. [[@ALGORITHM]] chooses the type of whatever you're looking at.
  4. [[@PRESETS]] always takes you home to the HOME page, from anywhere. If you've changed the sound, it protects you first (Lesson 1).

The keysJust over two octaves

The keys light up to show which notes are sounding, wherever they come from. You don't need to read music for this book. When a step says “a low note”, any key towards the left will do.

ConnectionsThe back edge

A little battery inside keeps the FM-1 going for hours. When it runs low, [[~BAT LOW]] appears at the top of the screen; plug in a USB charger. The GLOBAL page (Lesson 7) shows the exact percentage, and the firmware version too: check it reads {{FIRMWARE}} if you'd like everything to match this book.

It remembers where you wereA couple of seconds after you stop changing things, Shrub quietly keeps a copy of the sound you're working on. Switch off, switch on, and it's still there. That's a safety net, not a save: only [[SAVE]] keeps a sound in a slot of its own.

Understanding Shrub

Every sound Shrub makes takes the same journey. Once you can picture it, every page on the screen becomes a stop along the way, and the synth stops feeling like a maze.

The journeyFour stops

1 · Sources
Four things make raw sound. OSC A and OSC B are wavetable oscillators, the heart of Shrub. The SUB adds a simple wave, usually low, and NOISE adds hiss or rumble. Lesson 2.
2 · Filter
Removes part of the sound to change its colour: darker, brighter, thinner. Lesson 4.
3 · ENV 1
An envelope: it shapes each note's volume over time, so a sound can pluck, swell or linger. Lesson 3.
4 · FX RACK
Effects such as delay and reverb, which add space and character. Lesson 7.

Underneath runs modulation: slow-moving helpers (LFOs, extra envelopes) that turn knobs automatically, so a sound can drift and breathe on its own. They connect through a 16-slot routing table called the matrix. Lesson 5.

One thing that's differentAll notes share one filter

In most big synths each note gets its own filter. Shrub mixes the notes together first and then sends them through a single filter. On a single note you'll never notice. On chords, all the notes get darker or brighter together. That's partly why Shrub is so good at basses and lead lines, and it's why Lesson 4 builds a bass rather than a chord sound. The envelopes ENV 2 and ENV 3 and the four LFOs are shared the same way: one movement for every note, not one each.
