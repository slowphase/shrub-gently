01

Hello, Shrub

Switch on, browse the factory sounds, shape one with your hands, and save it as your own.

TimeAbout 30 minutes
Starts fromSwitching on
OutcomeYour first saved sound, [[~MY CHILL PAD]], in slot 101

The IdeaA machine that makes sound from scratch

A piano makes sound with hammers and strings; a guitar with plucked strings and a wooden body. A synthesiser makes sound electronically, from a few simple building blocks that you can reshape with knobs. Change the blocks and the same instrument can be a growling bass, a glassy bell or a gust of wind.

Setting dozens of knobs every time you wanted a sound would be tiring, so synths remember them. A preset (also called a patch) is a saved recipe: every setting, stored in one slot under one name. Shrub comes with 100 factory presets, sorted into seven categories: BASS, LEAD, MISC, PAD, PLUCK, SEQ and SYNTH.

Here's the freeing part. When you turn a knob, you change the sound that's playing, not the saved preset. The factory sound stays safe in its slot. Nothing becomes permanent until you press [[SAVE]], and even then Shrub steers you away from overwriting the original. So tonight, touch anything.

The HOME page

HOME is where Shrub starts and where [[@PRESETS]] always brings you. Its four knobs are the ones you'll reach for most:

  • CUTOFF makes the sound darker or brighter.
  • RES (resonance) adds a ringing edge.
  • A POS and B POS change the shape of the two oscillators' waves.

You'll learn exactly what each one does in Lessons 2 and 4. Tonight, just use your ears.

Let's Make SomethingYour first saved sound

ASwitch on and listen

  1. Turn [[@MASTER]] well down. Plug headphones or speakers into AUDIO OUT on the back edge (the built-in speaker switches off), or use the speaker for now.
  2. Slide the POWER switch on. Shrub shows its splash screen, a little tree, then lands on HOME. (Pressing any key or button skips the splash.)
  3. Play a few keys and slowly raise [[@MASTER]] until it's comfortable.

BBrowse the presets

  1. Turn [[@PRESETS]] one click at a time. The name changes straight away, and the sound loads a moment after you stop turning. Play a few notes on each.
  2. Now turn [[@ALGORITHM]] instead. Each click jumps to the first preset of the next category. Watch the title change: BASS, LEAD, MISC, PAD, PLUCK, SEQ, SYNTH.
  3. Spend five minutes wandering. Hold chords on the PAD presets; play short notes on PLUCK and BASS.
If a sound plays a patternSome presets, especially in SEQ, use the arpeggiator or recorded knob movements, so one held key can play a whole pattern. That's part of the sound. If movement carries on after you let go, turn [[@PRESETS]] to the next sound: the pattern belongs to the preset and stops with it. (Lesson 7 explains the arpeggiator and the [[PLAY]] button.)

CMove up and down

  1. Press [[OCT-]] twice and play: everything is lower. A bass preset can rumble like this. Press [[OCT+]] twice to return to the middle; both octave buttons go dark when you're back.

DShape a pad with your hands

  1. Turn [[@ALGORITHM]] until the title reads [[~PAD]]. You'll land on 042 PAD CHILL HAWK, the first pad.
  2. Hold a chord of three keys with one hand. With the other, turn [[@KNOB 1]] (CUTOFF) slowly anticlockwise. The sound darkens, as if a door is closing. Turn it back clockwise and the brightness returns.
  3. Turn [[@KNOB 2]] (RES) up a little and sweep CUTOFF again. Hear the whistling edge that follows the knob? Turn RES back down to where it was.
  4. Turn [[@KNOB 3]] (A POS) slowly while holding the chord. The tone changes character, sometimes subtly, sometimes a lot. This is the wavetable at work: Lesson 2 explains.
  5. Settle on something you like: a little darker and softer than the original is a good aim for a calm pad.

EMake it a favourite

  1. Hold [[HOME]] for about half a second. A star appears beside the title, [[~PAD *]]. Hold it again to remove the star; hold once more to put it back. Favourites become handy in Lesson 8.

FSave it under your own name

  1. Press [[SAVE]]. Because you've changed a factory preset, Shrub suggests the first empty slot rather than the original. The top of the screen reads [[~SAVE TO 101]] and [[~REPLACES INIT]]. (If it shows another number, that's the first empty slot on your FM-1: use it, and remember the number.)
  2. Now spell the name. [[@KNOB 2]] (CURSOR) chooses which letter position you're changing; [[@KNOB 3]] (LETTER) changes the letter. Spell [[~MY CHILL PAD]]. Names can be up to 16 characters.
  3. The page starts with the old name, sometimes with extra characters such as [[~[E]]] on the end. Move the cursor onto any leftover letters and turn LETTER until each position is blank (a space).
  4. Check [[@KNOB 4]] (CATEGORY) reads [[~PAD]].
  5. Press [[SAVE]] again. The screen says [[~SAVED]]. (Changed your mind? Any other section button, such as [[HOME]], leaves without saving.)

GProve it's safe

  1. Turn [[@PRESETS]] to a different sound, then back to 101. The numbers wrap around, so turning anticlockwise from 001 is the quick way to the high slots. Your pad is there, exactly as you left it, and its number now reads [[~U101]]: the U is Shrub's mark for a slot you've saved into.
EDITED, TURN AGAINBump [[@PRESETS]] after changing a sound and Shrub shows [[~EDITED]] / [[~TURN AGAIN]] instead of throwing your work away. Pause and turn again to move on, or press [[SAVE]] to keep it. It's a friend.

Have a PlayWander further

  • Visit every category with [[@ALGORITHM]] and find one sound in each that you like. Favourite them by holding [[HOME]].
  • Once you have favourites, [[@ALGORITHM]] on HOME also reaches a group called [[~FAVORITES]] (spelt the American way on screen), and [[@PRESETS]] then steps only through your starred sounds.
  • On any preset, turn CUTOFF right down and RES right up, then sweep CUTOFF slowly: one of the oldest tricks in electronic music.

What You've Learned

  • A synth builds sound from parts; a preset saves every setting.
  • [[@PRESETS]] browses; [[@ALGORITHM]] on HOME jumps categories.
  • HOME's four knobs: CUTOFF, RES, A POS, B POS.
  • [[SAVE]], spell with CURSOR and LETTER, [[SAVE]] again.

A calmer, softer take on a factory pad. Category PAD.

The Shape of Sound

What a sound wave is, why shape matters, and how a wavetable lets a wave change shape smoothly.

TimeAbout 40 minutes
Starts fromAn empty slot, [[~102 INIT]]
Outcome[[~FIRST WAVE]], a warm tone you built from nothing, in slot 102

The IdeaSound is a shape that repeats

Every sound is air wobbling back and forth. When a wobble repeats regularly, we hear a note. The part of a synth that makes this repeating wobble is called an oscillator (from the Latin for “to swing”).

Two things about the wobble matter most. The first is how fast it repeats, its frequency, measured in hertz (Hz): repeats per second. Faster means a higher note. That's pitch.

The second is the shape of each repeat. Two waves with the same pitch but different shapes sound completely different, just as a flute and a violin can play the same note. Synth-makers settled on a few classic shapes long ago:

Why corners sound bright

Any wave can be built by adding together pure, smooth sine waves at different speeds: one at the note's own frequency, called the fundamental, then others at two, three, four times that speed, and so on. These extra ingredients are called harmonics. A smooth wave has few of them and sounds soft. A wave with sharp corners and jumps needs lots of high harmonics, so it sounds bright and buzzy.

Hold onto that idea. In Lesson 4, the filter will work by removing harmonics.

Wavetables: a wave that can change shape

A simple oscillator plays one shape. A wavetable oscillator holds a whole stack of shapes, called frames, and can glide smoothly from one to the next. Shrub's oscillators A and B are wavetable oscillators, and the knob that picks the frame is called WT POS (wavetable position). On HOME, it's the same thing as A POS and B POS.

Shrub has 64 built-in wavetables. Each one is a different journey through sound: some move from soft to harsh, some from vowel to vowel, some through shapes that have no names at all.

An empty [[~INIT]] slot starts with one sound: oscillator A playing the first frame of a table called DEFAULT, which is a saw wave. Everything else is silent, and no effects are on. It's a blank canvas.

Let's Make SomethingA tone from nothing

AFind a blank canvas

  1. Turn [[@PRESETS]] until the screen shows 102 and the name [[~INIT]]. (Anticlockwise from 001 wraps round to 128, 127… which is the quick way. If 102 holds a sound of yours, use any slot still named INIT.)
  2. Play a note. That buzzy, plain sound is the saw wave, raw.
  3. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (CUTOFF) fully clockwise, to about [[~22.1 kHz]]. The sound gets brighter: you've opened the filter completely, so you hear the oscillators with nothing taken away. Lesson 4 is all about this knob.

BMeet the four classic shapes

The SUB oscillator can play the classic shapes on their own, which makes it a perfect listening lab.

  1. Press [[EDIT]] until the [[~OSC A]] pill is lit. Turn [[@SELECT]] anticlockwise until the first dot is lit (page 1).
  2. Turn [[@KNOB 4]] (LEVEL) fully anticlockwise to [[~0%]]. Oscillator A falls silent.
  3. Press [[EDIT]] twice more, past OSC B (already silent), until [[~SUB]] is lit.
  4. Turn [[@KNOB 3]] (SUB) up to about 70% and play a note. This is a sine: pure, round, a little like a soft whistle.
  5. Turn [[@ALGORITHM]] one click at a time, playing a note after each. The shape's name flashes up: [[~TRIANGLE]], [[~SQUARE]], [[~SAW]], then two extras, [[~ROUND RECT]] and [[~PULSE]]. Listen for the brightness growing as the corners get sharper.
  6. Turn [[@ALGORITHM]] back to [[~SINE]].
Listen forThe sine has no edge at all. The triangle has a faint hollowness. The square sounds woody, like a clarinet. The saw is the brightest, almost raspy. Each step adds harmonics.

CPitch is speed

  1. Still on SUB, turn [[@KNOB 2]] (OCT) one click clockwise and play the same key. It's an octave higher: the wave is repeating twice as fast. Try other values between −4 and +4, then return to [[~-1]], where it started.
  2. Play the lowest and highest keys. Same shape, very different speeds.

DTravel through a wavetable

  1. Turn the SUB level ([[@KNOB 3]]) back down to [[~0%]].
  2. Press [[EDIT]] until [[~OSC A]] is lit, and turn [[@KNOB 4]] (LEVEL) back up to [[~75%]].
  3. Hold a note and turn [[@KNOB 1]] (WT POS) slowly clockwise. The counter on the screen climbs from 1 to 9: the DEFAULT table has nine frames. Watch the shape in the display change as the sound changes.
  4. Now turn [[@ALGORITHM]] slowly. Each click loads a different wavetable and its name flashes up. Stop when the name reads [[~SAW>SQUARE]], just after [[~BASIC SHAPES]]. (If you overshoot, turn back: the names are the target, not the clicks.)
  5. This table has 64 frames. Hold a note and sweep WT POS slowly through all of them. Frame 1 is a saw; as you climb, the wave squares off and the tone grows hollower.
  6. Set WT POS to about 24, partway between bright and hollow.

EAdd a floor

  1. Press [[EDIT]] until [[~SUB]] is lit. Its shape should still be [[~SINE]] and OCT [[~-1]].
  2. Turn the SUB level ([[@KNOB 3]]) up to about 40%. Play low notes: the sine, an octave below, adds warmth and weight underneath. Many great sounds are built exactly like this: something interesting on top, something simple underneath.

FSave it

  1. Press [[SAVE]]. Because you started from slot 102, the page offers [[~SAVE TO 102]], [[~REPLACES INIT]].
  2. Spell [[~FIRST WAVE]] with [[@KNOB 2]] (CURSOR) and [[@KNOB 3]] (LETTER), blanking any leftover letters.
  3. Turn [[@KNOB 4]] (CATEGORY) to [[~SYNTH]].
  4. Press [[SAVE]] again: [[~SAVED]].

Have a PlayShapes and colours

  • Wander through the wavetables with [[@ALGORITHM]] on OSC A, sweeping WT POS on each. [[~DUDA CHOIR]] sounds like voices; the [[~RULE]] tables grow their frames from simple mathematical patterns.
  • On OSC A page 1, turn [[@KNOB 2]] (WARP) to choose a warp mode, then raise [[@KNOB 3]] (AMOUNT). Warps bend the wave as it plays. Try [[~BEND+]] and [[~SYNC]].
  • On OSC A page 3 ([[@SELECT]]), [[@KNOB 1]] OCT, [[@KNOB 2]] SEMI and [[@KNOB 3]] FINE retune the oscillator by octaves, semitones and tiny cents.
  • On SUB, raise [[@KNOB 4]] (NOISE). On SUB page 2, [[@KNOB 1]] (COLOR) chooses WHITE, PINK, BROWN, BLUE or CRUSH noise. Noise has no pitch at all: every frequency at once.
  • None of this changes slot 102 unless you save again.

What You've Learned

  • An oscillator makes a repeating wave. Speed is pitch; shape is tone.
  • Sharp corners mean more harmonics, and a brighter sound.
  • A wavetable is a stack of frames; WT POS moves through them.
  • [[EDIT]] steps OSC A, OSC B, SUB, FILTER. [[@ALGORITHM]] picks the table or shape.
  • Layering a simple low wave under a richer one adds warmth.
