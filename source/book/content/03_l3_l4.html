Lesson
03

Give It a Life

Every sound is born, lives and fades. Shape that life and a plain tone becomes a soft, plucked note.

TimeAbout 35 minutes
Starts fromAn empty slot, [[~103 INIT]]
Outcome[[~SOFT PLUCK]], a gentle plucked sound, in slot 103

The IdeaThe life of a note

Pluck a guitar string and listen closely. The sound jumps in instantly, rings, and fades away even while your finger stays put. Bow a violin and the sound swells in gently and stays as long as the bow moves. Same notes, different lives. That life, how loudness changes over time, is a huge part of what makes an instrument recognisable.

A synth shapes it with an envelope: think of it as an invisible hand on the volume knob that moves the same way every time you press a key. Shrub's envelopes have four main stages, known by their initials, ADSR:

ATTACK
How long the sound takes to reach full volume after you press a key. Short is a click or pluck; long is a swell.
DECAY
How long it then takes to fall to the sustain level.
SUSTAIN
A level, not a time: how loud the note stays while you keep holding the key. Shrub shows it in decibels. [[~0.0 dB]] is full volume; fully down is [[~-inf dB]], silence.
RELEASE
How long the sound takes to fade after you let go.

Just four knobs, but they cover an enormous range of characters:

Shrub has three envelopes. ENV 1 is permanently connected to volume, and every note gets its own copy, so each note you play has its own life. ENV 2 and ENV 3 are spares you can connect to other things; you'll use ENV 2 in Lesson 4.

The display draws the envelope's shape, and a small dot travels along it while a note plays, so you can see the stages happening. On an INIT slot, ENV 1 starts at [[~0.5 ms]] attack, [[~1.00 s]] decay, [[~0.0 dB]] sustain and [[~15 ms]] release.

Notice what that means: with sustain at full volume, the decay has nowhere to fall to. The note simply switches on and off, like an organ. You'll change that tonight.

Let's Make SomethingA soft pluck

AStart clean

  1. Turn [[@PRESETS]] to 103, [[~INIT]].
  2. Press [[ENV]] until [[~ENV 1]] is lit. Turn [[@SELECT]] anticlockwise to page 1.
  3. Hold a key for a couple of seconds, then let go. Watch the dot: it shoots up, stays at the top while you hold, and drops the instant you release. Organ-like.

BHear each stage on its own

  1. Attack. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (ATTACK) up to about 1 s. Hold a key: the sound now swells in. Turn it back down to the minimum, [[~0.5 ms]].
  2. Release. Turn [[@KNOB 4]] (RELEASE) up to about 2 s. Play short notes: each one lingers and fades after you let go. Lovely for pads, but blurry for fast playing.
  3. Sustain. Turn [[@KNOB 3]] (SUSTAIN) all the way anticlockwise, to [[~-inf dB]]. Now hold a key. The note fades by itself over about a second, even though you're still holding. That's the decay at work, falling all the way to silence.
Listen forWith sustain at silence, holding a key longer than the decay changes nothing: the note has already finished its life on its own. That's what makes a pluck, and plucked strings, struck bells and drums all behave like this. Letting go early still matters, though: the release takes over from wherever the note has reached, so a quick tap sounds shorter than a held note. You'll set that release next.

CShape the pluck

  1. Turn [[@KNOB 2]] (DECAY) down to about 350 ms (anything from 300 to 450 is fine). Play a few notes: short, plucky, bouncing.
  2. Set [[@KNOB 4]] (RELEASE) to about 250 ms. Notes you let go of early now fade instead of stopping dead, which sounds more natural.
  3. Play a slow melody, then a fast one. Adjust DECAY until it feels right: longer for a ringing harp, shorter for a muted, woody tap.

DSoften the tone

A raw saw is bright for a gentle pluck. Two skills from earlier lessons will soften it.

  1. Press [[HOME]]. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (CUTOFF) anticlockwise to about 1.2 kHz. The pluck becomes rounder, more like felt than wire.
  2. Press [[EDIT]] until [[~SUB]] is lit, and raise [[@KNOB 3]] (SUB) to about 30% for a little body underneath. Notice that the sub plucks too: ENV 1 shapes every source together.

ESave it

  1. Press [[SAVE]]. Check [[~SAVE TO 103]].
  2. Spell [[~SOFT PLUCK]], set CATEGORY to [[~PLUCK]], and press [[SAVE]] again.

Have a PlayOther lives

  • A swell: ATTACK about 1.5 s, SUSTAIN back up to [[~0.0 dB]], RELEASE about 2 s. Hold chords. The same oscillator is now a pad.
  • An organ: everything short, SUSTAIN at [[~0.0 dB]]. Notes on and off, nothing else.
  • On page 2 ([[@SELECT]]), [[@KNOB 3]] (D CURVE) bends the decay. Push it one way and the pluck snaps; the other way, it glows a little longer before fading.
  • Try the pluck through the [[~NICE PLUGGER]] wavetable on OSC A ([[EDIT]] until [[~OSC A]], then [[@ALGORITHM]]).
  • Remember: none of this changes slot 103 until you save.

What You've Learned

  • An envelope shapes a sound over time: attack, decay, sustain, release.
  • Sustain is a level; the other three are times.
  • No sustain plus a short decay makes a pluck.
  • ENV 1 is each note's volume; [[ENV]] steps through ENV 1, 2, 3 and the matrix.

Your CreationSOFT PLUCK

A saw with a short decay, no sustain, a soft release, a darker filter and a touch of sub. Category PLUCK.

Lesson
04

Dark and Bright

Start with something bright, then carve it into shape. Filters, resonance, and a warm, deep bass.

TimeAbout 45 minutes
Starts fromAn empty slot, [[~104 INIT]]
Outcome[[~DEEP BASS]], a round bass with a soft attack bloom, in slot 104

The IdeaSculpting by taking away

A sculptor starts with a block and removes everything that isn't the statue. Much of synthesis works the same way: start with a wave full of harmonics, like a saw, then remove some of them. This is called subtractive synthesis, and the tool that removes harmonics is a filter.

The most common filter is the low-pass. It lets the low harmonics pass through and removes the high ones. The point where it starts removing is called the cutoff. Turn the cutoff down and fewer harmonics get through: the sound turns darker, softer, more muffled. Turn it up and the brightness returns.

Resonance boosts the harmonics right at the cutoff, so the filter itself starts to sing. A little gives a sound edge and presence. A lot makes it whistle and squelch, especially as the cutoff moves.

A high-pass filter does the opposite: it removes the lows and keeps the highs. It's for thinning things out, like the tinny sound of a phone speaker.

Movement inside every note

A filter that stays still sounds fine. A filter that moves sounds alive. Shrub wires its second envelope, ENV 2, straight to the filter. Give ENV 2 a short decay and each note will start bright and settle darker, a little “bwow” that makes basses feel physical.

One Shrub detail matters here. Unlike ENV 1, there is only one ENV 2, shared by every note, just as there's one filter. A new note restarts it from wherever it is, and it releases only when the last key comes up. On a single line of notes, like tonight's bass, that's exactly what you want. On a held chord, the whole chord gets one sweep together, not one per note.

Page 1 is the main event. DRIVE pushes the sound into the filter harder, for warmth and grit. Page 2's ENV 2 sets how far ENV 2 moves the cutoff. Page 3 chooses which sources go through the filter at all: anything switched off skips it. The CUTOFF and RES knobs on HOME are the same two controls, kept handy.

Let's Make SomethingA deep bass

AHear the filter

  1. Turn [[@PRESETS]] to 104, [[~INIT]].
  2. Press [[EDIT]] until [[~FILTER]] is lit, and turn [[@SELECT]] to page 1. The type shows [[~LP 24]]: a low-pass filter.
  3. Hold a low note. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (CUTOFF) slowly all the way down, then all the way up. It runs from about 8 Hz, where almost nothing gets through, to [[~22.1 kHz]], wide open. Watch the curve on the screen follow you.
  4. Turn [[@KNOB 2]] (RES) up to about 60% and sweep CUTOFF again. Hear it whistle? Then bring RES back to about 30%.

BBuild the body

  1. Press [[EDIT]] until [[~OSC A]] is lit. Turn [[@SELECT]] to page 3 and set [[@KNOB 1]] (OCT) to [[~-1]]. The saw drops an octave.
  2. Press [[EDIT]] until [[~SUB]] is lit. Raise [[@KNOB 3]] (SUB) to about 50%. The sub is a sine set an octave below the keys, the same octave as the saw you just lowered. So it doesn't add a new, deeper note: it doubles the saw's lowest harmonic, the fundamental, making the bass round and heavy.

CSet the filter

  1. Press [[EDIT]] until [[~FILTER]] is lit, page 1.
  2. Set [[@KNOB 1]] (CUTOFF) to about 250 Hz. Dark and round.
  3. Set [[@KNOB 3]] (DRIVE) to about 20% for a little warmth.
  4. Turn [[@SELECT]] to page 3. Turn [[@KNOB 4]] (SUB) to [[~OFF]]. The sub now skips the filter, so the deep end stays solid whatever the filter does. A trick straight from the Operation Guide.

DMake it bloom

  1. Turn [[@SELECT]] to page 2. Turn [[@KNOB 3]] (ENV 2) clockwise to about 40%. Nothing much happens yet, because ENV 2 is still shaped like an organ.
  2. Press [[ENV]] until [[~ENV 2]] is lit, page 1.
  3. Turn [[@KNOB 3]] (SUSTAIN) all the way down to [[~-inf dB]], and [[@KNOB 2]] (DECAY) to about 300 ms.
  4. Play short low notes, one at a time. Each one opens with a soft “bwow” and settles into a round tone. That's ENV 2 sweeping the cutoff, exactly as in the diagram. (Hold one note and play another over it, and the second note won't bloom as much: the shared ENV 2 restarts from where it already was. Step 16 makes the bass play one note at a time.)
  5. Go back to the filter's page 2 ([[EDIT]] until [[~FILTER]], [[@SELECT]] to page 2) and adjust ENV 2 to taste. Less for subtle, more for funky.
Why the bloom mattersReal instruments are brighter at the start of a note, and the ear uses that brightness to hear where each note begins. Without it, a dark bass can feel smudged.

EOne note at a time

  1. Press [[GLO]] until [[~VOICING]] is lit. [[@KNOB 1]] reads [[~POLY]]. Turn it to [[~MONO]]. Now only one note plays at a time, like a real bass player, and a new note takes over from the last.
  2. For a gentle slide between notes, turn [[@KNOB 3]] (GLIDE) up to about 40 ms. Play two notes overlapping and hear the pitch glide.

FSave it

  1. Press [[SAVE]] (check [[~SAVE TO 104]]), spell [[~DEEP BASS]], set CATEGORY to [[~BASS]], and press [[SAVE]] again.

Have a PlayFilter flavours

  • On the filter's page 1, turn [[@ALGORITHM]] slowly and watch the type's name. Types starting with HP are high-pass; BP keeps only a band in the middle. [[~MG LADDER]], [[~MG DIRTY]] and [[~ACID LADDER]] imitate classic analogue filters. Come back to [[~LP 24]] if you get lost.
  • Raise RES to about 70% with ENV 2 at 60% for instant squelch, or push DRIVE past 60% for growl.
  • Play a chord on [[~MY CHILL PAD]] while sweeping CUTOFF on HOME. All the notes darken together: that's Shrub's one shared filter.

What You've Learned

  • A filter removes harmonics. Low-pass: darker. High-pass: thinner.
  • Cutoff sets where; resonance adds a peak at the cutoff.
  • ENV 2 moves the cutoff; in Shrub it's shared by all notes.
  • Sources can skip the filter (page 3).
  • MONO and GLIDE make a sound play like a single line.

A saw an octave down through a low, warm filter that blooms on each note, a sine sub doubling its fundamental, mono with a short glide. Category BASS.
