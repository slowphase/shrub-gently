Space and Motion

Put your pluck in a room, give it echoes, and let the arpeggiator play patterns from the notes you hold.

TimeAbout 40 minutes
Starts fromYour [[~SOFT PLUCK]] (slot 103)
Outcome[[~ECHO STEPS]], a spacious, moving pattern, in slot 107

The IdeaSpace, and patterns that play themselves

Clap in a small bathroom, then in a cathedral, then in an open field. Same clap, three different sounds, because the space answers back. Effects let you choose the space a sound lives in, along with a lot of other characters. Two matter most for atmospheric music:

  • Delay plays the sound back again after a short time, then again, each repeat quieter. FEEDBACK sets how many repeats; TIME sets the gap, and with BPM on it locks to the tempo so the echoes fall in rhythm. PING-PONG bounces them between left and right.
  • Reverb imitates a room. SIZE and DECAY make it bigger and longer; MIX sets how much of it you hear.

Shrub's effects sit in an FX RACK of up to seven slots, and the sound passes through them in order, top to bottom. An INIT slot's rack already holds DISTORTION, CHORUS, DELAY, COMPRESSOR and REVERB, all switched off, waiting. Your SOFT PLUCK started from an INIT slot, so its rack looks the same.

The arpeggiator

An arpeggio is a chord played one note at a time. An arpeggiator (arp) does this for you: hold a chord and it plays the notes in a pattern, in time with the tempo. Hold different notes and the pattern follows. The arp plays whenever it's switched on and you hold notes; it doesn't need the [[PLAY]] button.

Let's Make SomethingEcho steps

ALoad the pluck and set a tempo

  1. Turn [[@PRESETS]] to 103 SOFT PLUCK (it shows as [[~U103]]).
  2. Press [[GLO]] until [[~GLOBAL]] is lit, page 1. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (TEMPO) to [[~90 BPM]]: an unhurried pace. The echoes and the arp will both follow it.

BEchoes

  1. Press [[FX]] until the title reads [[~FX RACK]].
  2. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (SLOT) to 3, [[~DELAY]]. Turn [[@KNOB 4]] (MODE) clockwise until it reads [[~PING-PONG]] (it comes just after [[~NORMAL]]). The list shows [[~ON]].
  3. Play single short notes and leave gaps. The echoes bounce from side to side, in time with the tempo.
  4. Press [[FX]] until the title reads [[~DELAY]]. Its times start at [[~1/4]] (one beat). Turn [[@KNOB 2]] (TIME R) anticlockwise to [[~1/8]], so the right-hand echoes come twice as fast as the left. Rhythms tumble.
  5. Set [[@KNOB 3]] (FEEDBACK) to about 35% and [[@KNOB 4]] (MIX) to about 25%. The echoes should sit behind the notes, not compete with them.

CA room

  1. Press [[FX]] until [[~FX RACK]] is lit again. Turn SLOT to 5, [[~REVERB]], and turn MODE to [[~HALL]].
  2. Press [[FX]] until the title reads [[~REVERB]]. Set SIZE to about 60%, DECAY to about 55% and MIX to about 25%.
Too washy?Delay and reverb pile up quickly. If notes blur into a fog, lower the two MIX knobs before anything else.

DSwitch on the arp

  1. Press [[ARP]] to open it, then press [[ARP]] again to switch it on. The ARP button's light shows it's on, and the screen invites you to [[~HOLD NOTES TO ARPEGGIATE]].
  2. Hold three white keys a little apart, such as A, C and E. The arp plays them one at a time, a sixteenth note each. Change the keys you hold and the pattern follows.
  3. Turn [[@ALGORITHM]] to choose the pattern: try [[~UP/DOWN]]. Set [[@KNOB 3]] (OCTAVES) to [[~2]] and [[@KNOB 2]] (GATE) to about 60%, so each step is a short, separated pluck that leaves room for the echoes.
  4. Turn [[@SELECT]] to page 2 and set [[@KNOB 4]] (LATCH) to [[~ON]]. Now the arp keeps going after you let go, and a new chord replaces the old one. Sit back and listen to your pattern in its room.

ESave into a new slot, arp and all

Save while the pattern is still playing: the arp being on, its pattern, octaves, gate and latch, the tempo and the effects all go into the preset together. You started from one of your own sounds, so Shrub will offer to save over it. You want a new copy instead.

  1. Press [[SAVE]]. The page offers [[~SAVE TO 103]].
  2. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (SLOT) to 107. The top right should read [[~REPLACES INIT]]: an empty slot, nothing lost. (If it names a sound of yours, keep turning to a slot that says INIT.)
  3. Spell [[~ECHO STEPS]], set CATEGORY to [[~SEQ]], and press [[SAVE]] again.

FStop, and bring it back

  1. To stop the latched pattern, turn [[@PRESETS]] to another sound. The arp belongs to ECHO STEPS, so with ARP+SCALE on [[~PRESET]] (as it starts) it stops when another preset loads. Pressing [[ARP]] on the arp page to switch it off works too. ([[PLAY]] won't stop it: the arp doesn't run on the transport.)
  2. Turn back to 107 and hold three keys. The arp springs back exactly as you saved it. On HOME, the [[~ARP]] chip at the top right is lit: a quick sign that a preset has its arp switched on.

Your original [[~SOFT PLUCK]] is still waiting, untouched, in slot 103.

What saves, and what ARP+SCALE doesThe tempo, the arp and the scale (see Have a Play) are always saved with a preset. A separate setting of the FM-1 itself, ARP+SCALE on the GLOBAL page, decides what happens when you change presets. On [[~PRESET]], as it starts, each preset brings its own arp, key, scale and chord. On [[~GLOBAL]], whatever arp and scale you're using carry on as you browse other sounds. Leave it on PRESET for this book.

Have a PlayScales and motion

  • Lock to a scale. Press [[SCL]]. Turn [[@KNOB 1]] (KEY) to [[~A]] and [[@KNOB 2]] (SCALE) to [[~Minor]]. The keys light up to show the notes of A minor, and any key you press, black or white, is nudged to the nearest one. That doesn't make every combination pretty, but it keeps you inside one family of notes, which is a lovely way to explore. Set KEY to [[~--]] to switch it off.
  • In SCL, [[@KNOB 3]] (CHORD) turns each key into a whole chord. Combine it with the arp.
  • Try other arp patterns: [[~CONVERGE]], [[~RANDOM]], [[~PINKY UP]]. On page 1, [[@KNOB 4]] (CHANCE) makes some steps skip.
  • Record a knob move. With the arp latched, press [[REC]] (it starts the transport), go to HOME and turn CUTOFF slowly down and up over a few seconds, then stop turning and press [[REC]] again. Shrub's motion sequencer loops your movement in a lane, and the [[SEQ]] light comes on. To remove it, press [[SEQ]], turn [[@SELECT]] to the lane holding your movement, turn [[@KNOB 1]] (POINT) to [[~CLEAR LANE]] and press [[SEQ]]. ([[~CLEAR ALL]] also works, but it wipes every lane in the sound you're working on, and asks you to press [[SEQ]] again to confirm.) Unlike the arp, lanes keep playing when you load a preset that has none of its own; [[PLAY]] stops them, and CLEAR LANE removes them, so do this before moving on.

What You've Learned

  • Delay makes echoes; reverb makes a room.
  • FX RACK: SLOT picks, MODE switches on; [[FX]] steps through pages.
  • The arp plays held notes as patterns, without the transport.
  • Arp, tempo and effects save with the preset.
  • Saving to a different SLOT makes a new copy.

Your First Sound Bank

Three more sounds from your own recipes, a tidy bank of ten, and a first recording into the MPC Sample.

Time60 to 90 minutes: happily split over two sittings
Starts fromSlots 108 to 110, still [[~INIT]]
OutcomeA finished bank of ten sounds, slots 101 to 110

The IdeaThinking like a sound designer

Professional sound designers rarely start by turning knobs at random. They start with a picture: a cold wind across a hillside, a bell heard underwater. Then they ask five questions, and you now know how to answer every one:

QuestionWhere you answer it
What is it made of?Sources: wavetable, sub, noise (Lesson 2)
How does it live and die?Envelopes (Lesson 3)
Is it dark or bright?The filter (Lesson 4)
Does it move, and how?LFOs and unison (Lessons 5 and 6)
What space does it live in?Effects (Lesson 7)

A sound bank is a collection that works together: named clearly, sorted into categories, quick to find. Tonight you'll add three sounds from short recipes, try one sound of your own with no recipe at all, then tidy the whole set. A natural place to pause is after the recipes.

Optional challengeA sound with no recipe

The recipes proved you can follow instructions. This proves you understand them. There's no right answer, and nothing here can affect your ten saved sounds.

  1. Imagine first, hands off the FM-1. Pick a sound you can picture clearly: a ship's horn in fog, a music box in another room, rain on a tin roof, or one of your own. Say it out loud, or write it down.
  2. Answer the five questions on paper. Which source makes it: a wavetable, the sub, noise? How does its volume change over time: pluck, swell, steady? Bright or dark? Does it move, and how fast? What kind of space does it live in?
  3. Build it in the next slot still named [[~INIT]] (111 if your bank is untouched), working through your answers in order.
  4. Compare what you hear with what you imagined. Which question did you answer wrongly? Change that one thing, listen again, and repeat.
  5. Save it under a name of your choosing, checking that the SAVE page says [[~REPLACES INIT]].
If it doesn't matchThat's the useful part. Getting from “not quite” to “yes, that” is the skill. Most fixes are one of four moves: a different source, a different envelope, the filter, or less effect.

DTidy the bank

  1. Visit each of your ten slots, 101 to 110, with [[@PRESETS]]. Play each one for a moment. Does its name still describe it? Is its category right?
  2. To rename or re-categorise a sound: load it, press [[SAVE]] (it opens on its own slot), change the name or CATEGORY, and press [[SAVE]] again.
  3. Hold [[HOME]] on each of your sounds to star it as a favourite. If a title already shows a star, leave it: holding again would remove it.
  4. Optional: if you starred factory presets earlier (such as 042 PAD CHILL HAWK in Lesson 1), they're still favourites. To make FAVORITES hold only your own bank, load each one and hold [[HOME]] again to remove its star. Removing a star changes nothing else: the sound stays exactly where it was.
  5. Press [[HOME]] and turn [[@ALGORITHM]] until you reach [[~FAVORITES]]. Now [[@PRESETS]] steps through only your starred sounds: your own instrument, ready to play. Favourites are entirely optional; skip them and your bank is just as safe in slots 101 to 110.
A copy for safekeeping (optional, needs a computer)Shrub's browser editor at shrub.audio/editor can back up everything on your FM-1. In Chrome or Edge, connect the FM-1 with USB, press Connect FM-1, open the LIBRARY tab and use Get from FM-1, then save the bank to your computer from the Bank menu. The file (a .shrubbank) holds all 128 slots, their wavetables and your MIDI map, so it's a complete copy of your sounds.

OptionalRecord a Shrub sound into the MPC Sample

Your sounds can leave the FM-1 and become samples you chop, pitch and sequence on the MPC. If you've worked through MPC, Gently, this is its Lesson 6 recording method with a cable instead of the microphone.

  1. Turn the FM-1's [[@MASTER]] down. You need a 3.5 mm TRS stereo to dual 6.35 mm TS mono breakout cable: one small stereo plug at one end, two larger 1/4 inch mono plugs at the other. Plug the 3.5 mm stereo plug into the FM-1's AUDIO OUT. On the MPC Sample's back, plug the left mono plug (marked L, often white or black) into AUDIO IN 1/L and the right mono plug (marked R, usually red) into AUDIO IN 2/R.
  2. On the MPC, press [[SAMPLE]] and choose an empty bank with [[PAD BANK]].
  3. Press [[SAMPLE RECORD]]. Set [[@K1]] (Source) to [[~Rear]], which records both AUDIO IN sockets in stereo; [[@K2]] to [[~Free]]; and [[@K3]] (Threshold) all the way down. Press [[B1]] until Monitor reads [[~Auto]].
  4. On the FM-1, load [[~WIDE SKY]] and play a chord, bringing [[@MASTER]] up until you hear it through the MPC. Watch the MPC's meters: aim for strong but never red. If they light red, turn the FM-1's MASTER down, or the REC GAIN knob on the MPC's back; if they barely move, turn up. Set it by eye and ear: there's no single right number.
  5. Tap an empty pad to start recording. Play one chord on the FM-1 and hold it; let go and wait for the release to fade completely. Tap the pad again to stop.
  6. Press [[SAMPLE]] and tap the pad. Your Shrub chord is now an MPC sample, ready to trim and chop.
Good habits for sampling synthsRecord a little silence before and after each sound; you can trim it later. One recorded note is one sample: it doesn't spread itself across the pads at different pitches by itself. To play a bass or pluck melodically, record a long note from the middle of the keyboard and repitch it on the MPC (16 Levels in Tune mode does this, as in MPC, Gently), or record each note you need. For ECHO STEPS, set the FM-1's TEMPO to match your MPC project so the loop chops cleanly on the beat.

Have a PlayKeep going

  • Do the challenge again with a harder picture: a submarine's sonar, a door creaking open in space.
  • Take a factory preset apart: load it and walk through EDIT, ENV, LFO and FX to see how it was made. You'll recognise almost everything now.
  • Make a variation: load one of your sounds, change one thing, and save it to the next empty slot with a name like [[~DEEP BASS 2]].

What You've Learned

  • Five questions turn an imagined sound into settings.
  • A recipe lists only what changes from INIT.
  • Rename, re-categorise and star sounds to keep a bank tidy.
  • FAVORITES turns your bank into your own instrument.
  • AUDIO OUT into the MPC's AUDIO IN turns a patch into a sample.
