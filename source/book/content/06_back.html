When Things Go Wrong

Almost every surprise has a simple cause. Find the closest match below.

There's no sound
Check
  1. [[@MASTER]] is up, and headphones are fully plugged into AUDIO OUT (with nothing plugged in, the speaker plays).
  2. The preset's own LEVEL: [[GLO]] until [[~GLOBAL]], [[@KNOB 2]]. An INIT slot starts at [[~-6.0 dB]].
  3. On GLOBAL page 3, USB LEVEL: [[~SPK OFF]] silences the speaker and jack on purpose (for monitoring through a computer).
  4. In your own sound: is every source's LEVEL at 0%? Is CUTOFF right down? Is ENV 1's attack very long, or sustain at [[~-inf dB]] with a tiny decay?
The sound keeps changing on its own
Cause
Something is modulating it: an LFO, chaos, or a recorded knob movement in the motion sequencer.
Fix
  1. Look for blue rings on the screen's knobs: those are being moved.
  2. If the [[SEQ]] button is lit, the sound has recorded lanes. Loading a preset that has no lanes leaves them running, so clear them: press [[SEQ]], pick the lane with [[@SELECT]], turn POINT to [[~CLEAR LANE]] and press [[SEQ]]. [[~CLEAR ALL]] (press [[SEQ]] twice) wipes every lane in the current sound.
  3. Check each LFO's AMOUNT, or look in the MATRIX for active slots.
Notes keep playing after I let go
Cause
A long RELEASE, the arp with LATCH on, recorded motion lanes, or a stuck note from MIDI.
Fix
  1. A latched arp: turn [[@PRESETS]] to another sound, or press [[ARP]] on the arp page to switch it off. [[PLAY]] doesn't stop the arp.
  2. Shorten ENV 1's RELEASE.
  3. [[PLAY]] lit and blinking? Press it to stop the transport, which plays recorded lanes.
  4. Still stuck: change preset, or send “all notes off” (the editor's panic button does this).
PRESETS won't change the sound
Cause
You've edited the sound, so Shrub shows [[~EDITED]] / [[~TURN AGAIN]] to protect it.
Fix
Press [[SAVE]] to keep the edits, or pause and turn [[@PRESETS]] again to leave them behind.
I saved, but I can't find my sound
Cause
It went into the slot shown on the SAVE page, which for a changed factory preset is the first empty INIT slot.
Fix
  1. Turn [[@PRESETS]] through 101 to 128. Every slot you've saved into shows a U before its number (U101, or U007 for a factory slot you saved over).
  2. Next time, read the top line of the SAVE page before pressing [[SAVE]] again.
The SAVE page shows a message after saving
Meaning
  1. [[~SAVED]]: all good.
  2. [[~SAVED, THINNED]] or [[~SAVED, NO LANES]]: recorded knob movements were too detailed to fit, and were smoothed or left out.
  3. [[~SAVED, AUX CUT]], [[~LFOS CUT]] or [[~NO LFO 4]]: a very big patch lost a little modulation detail (an aux routing, or an LFO's drawn shape) to fit its slot. The saved copy may sound slightly different from what you heard: reload it and listen. If it matters, simplify the patch, for example by removing a routing you don't need, and save again.
  4. [[~NOT SAVED: STORAGE FULL]]: preset memory is full; save over an old slot you no longer need, or free space in the editor's LIBRARY. Nothing was saved, so don't leave the sound yet.
I'm lost in the menus
Fix
Read the pills at the top: they show which part you're in. Press the section button repeatedly to cycle round, and turn [[@SELECT]] anticlockwise to get back to page 1. When in doubt, press [[HOME]]: your sound isn't affected.
The keys play the wrong notes
Cause
An octave shift, a scale, or chord mode.
Fix
  1. A lit [[OCT-]] or [[OCT+]] means you're shifted; press the other to return.
  2. Press [[SCL]]: KEY set to a note snaps everything to a scale. Set KEY to [[~--]] to switch it off.
  3. CHORD (also on SCL) set to anything but [[~OFF]] turns keys into chords.
The battery or the screen
Meaning
[[~BAT LOW]] at the top: under 30%, so charge over USB-C. A red screen with a pattern on it is a crash report: it restarts by itself after about ten seconds (a photo of it helps the developer). [[~WATCHDOG RESET]] on GLOBAL means Shrub restarted itself after a hang.
It won't start properly
Fix
  1. Shrub protects itself: after repeated failed starts it skips your user presets, then starts without its saved data, then goes into recovery on its own.
  2. To enter USB recovery mode yourself: connect the USB cable to a computer, hold [[OCT-]] and [[OCT+]], and switch on. Keep holding until the screen says [[~USB RECOVERY]]. Then run the official installer at shrub.audio again.
Installing and updating safelyUse only the official installer at shrub.audio/editor/install, in Chrome or Edge, with other music software closed. Don't touch the cable until it says it's done. If it stops part way, run it again: it carries on. Updating keeps your user presets and wavetables. M-VAVE's own updater takes you back to the original firmware whenever you like. This book doesn't recommend any unofficial shortcuts.

Glossary

ADSR
Attack, decay, sustain, release: the four stages of an envelope.
Algorithm (knob)
Shrub's “type” knob: wavetable, filter type, mode or pattern.
Arpeggiator
Plays the notes you hold one at a time, in a pattern, in time.
Attack
How long a sound takes to reach full volume.
Bipolar
Modulation that swings both ways from a setting, not just one.
BPM
Beats per minute: tempo.
Category
One of seven labels for presets: BASS, LEAD, MISC, PAD, PLUCK, SEQ, SYNTH.
Cent
A hundredth of a semitone. A few cents of detune makes beating.
Cutoff
Where a filter starts removing harmonics.
Decay
How long a sound takes to fall from its peak to the sustain level.
Delay
An effect that repeats the sound as echoes.
Destination
The knob a modulation source moves.
Detune
Tuning copies of a sound slightly apart.
Drive
Pushing a signal harder for warmth or grit.
Envelope
A shape over time, started by each note, that moves a setting.
Filter
Removes some frequencies to change a sound's colour.
Frame
One single-cycle wave in a wavetable.
Frequency
How many times a wave repeats each second, in hertz (Hz).
Fundamental
The lowest harmonic: the note's own frequency.
Glide
A slide in pitch from one note to the next.
Harmonics
The extra, higher sine waves that give a sound its tone.
High-pass
A filter that keeps highs and removes lows.
INIT
An empty, plain starting sound.
LFO
Low-frequency oscillator: a slow wave used to move other settings.
Low-pass
A filter that keeps lows and removes highs.
Matrix
Shrub's 16-slot table of modulation routings.
Modulation
One part of a synth automatically moving another.
Mono
One note at a time.
Noise
A sound with every frequency at once and no pitch.
Octave
The same note, twice (or half) the frequency.
Oscillator
The part of a synth that makes a repeating wave.
Paraphonic
Several notes sharing one filter, as in Shrub.
Patch, preset
A saved sound: every setting, in one slot.
Pitch
How high or low a note sounds.
Poly
Several notes at once.
Release
How long a sound takes to fade after you let go.
Resonance
A boost at the filter's cutoff that makes it ring.
Reverb
An effect that imitates the reflections of a room.
Sample
A recording of a sound, as on the MPC.
Scale
A set of notes that sound good together; Shrub can snap to one.
Sub
Shrub's simple extra oscillator, usually set low.
Sustain
The level a note holds while the key is down.
Transport
Shrub's play/stop clock for recorded lanes and the metronome ([[PLAY]]).
Unison
Several copies (voices) of one oscillator playing together.
Voice
One copy of a sound being played.
Wavetable
A stack of frames an oscillator can move through.
WT POS
Wavetable position: which frame plays.

Where Next

You now know the ideas that sit under nearly every synthesiser ever made. Here are some directions to grow.

Go deeper on the FM-1

  • The matrix. Route ENV 3, the CHAOS sources or the four MACROS to anything. One macro can move several knobs at once.
  • Warp modes. Each oscillator has 27 warp modes that bend its wave, from SYNC to FM. Pair a warp with an LFO.
  • The motion sequencer. Record knob moves and notes into eight looping lanes, and save them with the sound.
  • Chord mode and scales. 51 scales and a chord player on the SCL page.
  • The Operation Guide at shrub.audio/guide covers every page, knob and hidden move.

The browser editor

The editor at shrub.audio/editor shows every knob on one screen and, when your FM-1 is connected by USB in Chrome or Edge, edits it live. It's where you can draw LFO shapes, drag modulation onto knobs, bring in your own wavetables (WAV files with 2,048 samples per frame), import Serum 1 presets, and back up banks. Nothing in this book needed it.

Shrub and the MPC Sample

  • Sample, then play. A single recorded note is just one sample at one pitch. To play it melodically across the pads, repitch it on the MPC (for example with 16 Levels in Tune mode), or record each note you need.
  • Resample textures. Record a minute of SLOW TIDE or NIGHT DRONE, then chop the best moments into pads.
  • Match the tempo. Set Shrub's TEMPO to your MPC project's BPM before recording arps and echoes.
  • Play Shrub from the MPC. Shrub listens to MIDI on its 3.5 mm TRS MIDI IN, and the MPC Sample has a TRS MIDI output. Before buying a cable, check which TRS wiring (type A or B) each end expects. Sequencing the FM-1 from the MPC is a whole book of its own.
